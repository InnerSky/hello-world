# Foundation: creating the conditions for transformation

This page sets the architecture of every experience. The other library pages (lenses, practices,
arcs) supply the ingredients: what humanity's traditions and best practitioners know works. This page
says what the ingredients are *for* and how they're put together.

The frame comes from *Designing & Leading Life-Changing Workshops* by Ken Nelson, David Ronka,
Lesli Lang, Liz Korabek-Emerson and Jim White. Inner Sky treats each daily experience as a one-person
retreat built on their principles.

## Three models: knowing, doing, being

| Model | Focus | Objective | In an experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional | **knowing** | information is understood | a line of framing, a lineage, a "why" |
| Experiential | **doing** | something is practiced | breath, movement, writing, ritual |
| Transformational | **being** | the person perceives themselves, others or their world differently | self-inquiry that leads to a shift in self-perception |

Weave all three, but aim at **being**. Knowing and doing serve it. The question that leads every
design: *how might this person be different, or see differently, because of these twenty minutes?*

## Principles

1. **Objectives are about identity.** Every Reading names a *being objective*: a shift in how the
   person perceives themselves, others or the world. ("From someone who needs a reason to enjoy,
   toward someone who simply enjoys.")
2. **Create conditions; don't predict outcomes.** You can't know what someone needs for their growth.
   You can direct their attention to their own needs, and make it safe and interesting to look. The
   designer (and the companion) is the guardian of the container, not the author of the insight.
3. **Self-awareness makes the person the agent of their own learning.** The book puts it this way:
   "Through self-awareness, she became an agent for her own learning." So: ask rather than tell.
   Questions, probes and invitations to notice do more than explanations. The most powerful moments
   in practice have been self-inquiry and reflection.
4. **Needs-based and person-centered.** The intake is theirs, the pace is theirs, and every step can
   be adapted. Freedom is part of the method, not a courtesy.
5. **The whole person: mind, body, heart and spirit.** Every experience touches all four. Body work
   *sets the stage*: it regulates, opens and prepares the person for inquiry to land.
6. **Reflection and processing are part of the learning, not breaks from it.** After each block of
   direct experience comes a moment to process it: notice, name, write, choose, or hear a reflection.
   Rhythm: *experience → reflect → make meaning*.
7. **Curiosity and openness.** Explore through both information and direct experience. Invite
   curiosity about what happens, including resistance, tears, nothing at all.
8. **New meaning is the harvest.** "Fostering self-awareness provides opportunities to make new meaning
   from experience." Every experience ends with the person putting the new meaning into their own words
   (a decision, an anchor, a sentence), which becomes the card they keep.

## How this shapes the design

- **The Reading** (compass.md) includes the being objective.
- **The arc** (arcs.md) alternates experience and processing, and the Turn is usually self-inquiry
  made safe by the body work before it.
- **The craft tests** (craft.md) include a transformation test: does the person discover something
  themselves, rather than being told it?
- **Integration** asks how they see themselves now, not only what they liked.
