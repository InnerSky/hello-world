# Teachings

Short teachings an experience can offer at the right moment: one idea, a distinction or two, and
where it comes from. A teaching is *knowing in service of being* (see `foundation.md`): it gives
words to something the person has just felt, or reframes what they're about to try. It never
replaces the experience.

## How to use one

- **One per experience, two at most.** Place it where the person is receptive: right after a
  practice has opened something, or just before a reflection that the idea can light up. Not at
  the start, when nothing has been felt yet.
- **Short.** A title, up to 4 lines, up to 2 distinctions. If it needs more, it's a lecture.
- **Offered, not imposed.** "Try this on" rather than "this is the truth". Let them disagree.
- **Paraphrase and attribute.** Quote exactly only a single short line whose wording you're sure
  of (marked "exact" below); otherwise put it in your own words and say whose idea it is. Never
  invent a quote. Watch for famous misattributions (one is noted under *Meaning*).
- **Make it theirs.** Tie the last line to what they said today, with `{{stepId}}` echoes where
  they fit. The teaching should feel chosen for this morning, not pulled from a list.
- **Follow it with doing or reflecting.** A distinction lands when the next step lets them test
  it: a practice, a choice between the two sides, or a question.

Step format: `{"type": "teaching", "title", "lines", "distinctions": [{"not", "is"}], "source"}`
(see `engine/SCHEMA.md`). The title and lines are narrated; distinctions are read on screen.

## The teachings

### Gratitude
- **When:** after a practice that opened warmth or noticing; when they feel they "should" be grateful.
- **Core:** Gratitude has two parts: noticing that something is good, and seeing that it came at
  least partly from outside yourself, from a person, a body, a morning. It grows from specifics,
  not from lists.
- **Distinctions:** not *owing someone* / but *receiving something* · not *"I should be grateful"* /
  but *noticing one thing that is actually good*.
- **Confusion:** that gratitude means pretending things are fine. It sits alongside difficulty;
  it doesn't cancel it.
- **Source:** Robert Emmons, *Thanks!* (2007); David Steindl-Rast: it is gratefulness that makes
  us happy, not the other way round (paraphrase of his well-known line).

### Enjoyment and pleasure
- **When:** they want more joy; they feel guilty about pleasure; after play or movement.
- **Core:** Pleasure restores us: food, rest, comfort. Enjoyment involves us: attention fully in,
  something a little new, and coming out a bit more ourselves. Both are good. Enjoyment is the one
  that grows us.
- **Distinctions:** not *getting something* / but *being fully in something* · not *a reward for
  later* / but *a way of doing it now*.
- **Confusion:** that enjoyment has to be earned or justified.
- **Source:** Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, *Flow* (1990).

### Savoring
- **When:** after something good happened, in the session or in their life; before the card.
- **Core:** Good moments pass through us quickly unless we stay with them. Savoring is staying a
  few breaths longer: noticing, sharing it, letting it be felt in the body. We can savor ahead
  (anticipating), now, and looking back.
- **Distinctions:** not *grasping to keep it* / but *letting it fully land*.
- **Source:** Fred Bryant and Joseph Veroff, *Savoring* (2007).

### Permission to enjoy
- **When:** they feel they need a reason to enjoy, rest or play; productivity guilt.
- **Core:** We don't play music to reach the last note, or dance to arrive somewhere on the floor.
  The point of the dance is the dancing. A life can be lived the same way.
- **Distinctions:** not *enjoyment as the reward at the end* / but *enjoyment as the way through*.
- **Source:** Alan Watts, from his talks on music and life (paraphrase).

### Rest
- **When:** tired, wired, behind on everything; resting feels like losing.
- **Core:** Rest is not a reward for finishing, or fuel for more work. It's part of being a person.
  Deliberate rest is a skill: it has to be chosen, protected, and practiced.
- **Distinctions:** not *collapse or numbing out* / but *rest that restores* · not *earned* /
  but *owed to you by being alive*.
- **Source:** Tricia Hersey, *Rest Is Resistance* (2022); Alex Soojung-Kim Pang, *Rest* (2016).

### Self-compassion
- **When:** self-critical, ashamed, "I should be better at this".
- **Core:** Treat yourself the way you'd treat a good friend who is struggling: kindly, remembering
  that struggling is part of being human, and seeing the pain clearly without inflating it.
- **Distinctions:** not *self-pity* / but *kindness that includes everyone who struggles* · not
  *lowering the bar* / but *the support that makes trying again possible*.
- **Confusion:** that self-compassion makes you soft or lazy. Research suggests it supports
  motivation to improve.
- **Source:** Kristin Neff, *Self-Compassion* (2011); Breines and Chen (2012).

### Acceptance
- **When:** fighting a feeling, a situation or their body; stuck in "this shouldn't be happening".
- **Core:** Acceptance is letting what is already here be here, clearly and kindly, so your energy
  can go to what you care about instead of to the fight with reality.
- **Distinctions:** not *approval or giving up* / but *starting from what's true* · not
  *liking it* / but *making room for it*.
- **Source:** Tara Brach, *Radical Acceptance* (2003); Steven Hayes, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy.

### Letting go
- **When:** holding on to a plan, a grudge, an outcome; after a release practice.
- **Core:** Letting go a little brings a little peace; letting go a lot brings a lot of peace.
  It isn't something you do with force. It's loosening your grip and noticing that you can.
- **Distinctions:** not *pushing away* / but *loosening the grip* · not *ceasing to care* /
  but *caring without clutching*.
- **Source:** Ajahn Chah (paraphrase of a teaching recorded by his students).

### Impermanence
- **When:** savoring something precious; grief; a good season they fear losing.
- **Core:** Ajahn Chah, holding a glass he loved, said that to him it was already broken. Knowing it
  would one day break, he could enjoy it completely now, and when it broke, that was simply how it was.
- **Distinctions:** not *nothing matters because it ends* / but *it's precious because it ends*.
- **Source:** Ajahn Chah, as told by Mark Epstein in *Thoughts Without a Thinker* (1995).

### Play
- **When:** heavy, over-serious, flat; before or after a playful practice.
- **Core:** Play is done for its own sake, freely, and while we're in it we lose track of time and
  of ourselves. Adults need it as much as children do.
- **Exact:** "The opposite of play is not work. It is depression." (Brian Sutton-Smith)
- **Distinctions:** not *a break from what matters* / but *one of the things that matters*.
- **Source:** Stuart Brown, *Play* (2009); Brian Sutton-Smith, *The Ambiguity of Play* (1997).

### Attention
- **When:** scattered, on screens; after a noticing practice; when they want more meaning in the ordinary.
- **Core:** What we pay attention to becomes our life. Attention given fully to anything (a cup of
  tea, a person, a morning) becomes a kind of care.
- **Exact:** "Attention is the beginning of devotion." (Mary Oliver, *Upstream*)
- **Distinctions:** not *concentrating harder* / but *letting something have you for a moment*.
- **Source:** Mary Oliver; Simone Weil on attention as generosity.

### Meaning
- **When:** going through something hard; asking what it's all for.
- **Core:** Meaning is found in three places: in what we create or give, in what we experience and
  love, and in the stance we take toward what we can't change. That last freedom can't be taken away.
- **Distinctions:** not *a meaning to discover once* / but *meaning made, one choice at a time*.
- **Source:** Viktor Frankl, *Man's Search for Meaning* (1946). Careful: "between stimulus and
  response there is a space" is widely attributed to Frankl but isn't his; don't use it as his.

### Effortless action
- **When:** trying too hard, including at relaxing, meditating or enjoying.
- **Core:** Some things come only when we stop forcing them: sleep, spontaneity, joy. Wu wei is
  acting in step with things, like water that finds its way without pushing.
- **Distinctions:** not *doing nothing* / but *not forcing* · not *trying to relax* / but
  *letting go of the trying*.
- **Source:** Laozi, *Daodejing*; Edward Slingerland, *Trying Not to Try* (2014).

### Leaning in
- **When:** something uncomfortable is here; anxiety; wanting to escape a feeling.
- **Core:** When things fall apart, the habit is to run from the sharp edges. The practice is to
  stay a little longer, get curious, and find that the edge is softer than the story about it.
- **Distinctions:** not *toughing it out* / but *staying, gently, a moment longer*.
- **Source:** Pema Chödrön, *When Things Fall Apart* (1997).

### Joy as a decision
- **When:** they've said they want to decide to be happy; waiting for circumstances to change first.
- **Core:** Joy doesn't have to wait for life to be arranged. How we are inside is, more than we
  think, something we can take responsibility for and choose, again each morning.
- **Distinctions:** not *pretending to be happy* / but *deciding not to wait to be* · not *never
  feeling sad* / but *not handing your joy to circumstances*.
- **Confusion:** that deciding to be happy means suppressing what's hard. It includes it.
- **Source:** Sadhguru, *Inner Engineering* (2016).

### Being alive
- **When:** flat, numb, going through the motions; after an enlivening practice.
- **Core:** Perhaps what we're looking for isn't so much a meaning for life as the experience of
  being alive: feeling it in the body, here, now.
- **Source:** Joseph Campbell, *The Power of Myth* (1988), paraphrase.
