# Arcs: the shape of an experience

An experience is a small rite of passage. Anthropologists describe three phases: separation from
ordinary time, a liminal middle where change is possible, and return (Arnold van Gennep, Victor
Turner). The engine's default arc of six movements follows that shape.

| Movement | Job | Typical share of time |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrive** | cross the threshold; feel met; measure where you start | 5–10% |
| **Settle** | regulate enough that the rest can land | 15–20% |
| **Meet** | turn toward what's here, with curiosity | 20–25% |
| **Turn** | the shift: a new relationship, view or permission | 25–30% |
| **Integrate** | let it land in the body; give it a form | 10% |
| **Return** | a next step, a pocket practice, measure, close | 10% |

Movements can be renamed, merged or added to fit the experience ("Harbor", "Clearing",
"Threshold"), but the job of each phase stays.

## Principles of pacing

1. **Safety before depth.** Settle before Meet. Always.
2. **Pendulation.** Alternate between the hard and the resourced. After every difficult step, offer
   a breath, a resource or a widening.
3. **Titration.** Small doses. One feeling, one part, one thought at a time.
4. **One throughline.** A single image or metaphor carries the whole passage (weather, a river, a
   house with many rooms, setting down a pack). The skies should follow it.
5. **Rhythm.** Vary the mode: read → breathe → write → sit → choose. Never more than two writing
   steps in a row, never more than about 90 seconds of reading in a row.
6. **The peak sits at roughly two-thirds.** The Turn is the high point. Afterwards, everything
   gets lighter and simpler.
7. **End lighter than the middle, with agency.** The last steps are about what *they* choose and carry.
8. **Respond, don't just script.** Use at least one choice point that changes what comes next
   (`when`), and echo what they wrote (`{{stepId}}`) so the experience is clearly listening.
9. **One surprise.** Include one moment they wouldn't have predicted: a probe, a playful reframe,
   a letter from an unexpected voice, an awe moment. Novelty keeps the experience alive.
10. **Skies follow feeling.** Move through the sky states the way the inner weather should move,
    gradually. Keep the ink family (dark or light text) mostly stable within an experience.

## Arc patterns

Starting shapes, not templates. Each lists movements → typical practices.

**The Clearing** (overwhelm, too much, scattered) · Settle-heavy
Arrive (mirror, weather, scale) → Settle (cyclic sigh, orienting) → Meet (clearing a space, body map)
→ Turn (release ritual, sit in the space, "what matters most?") → Integrate (taking in the good)
→ Return (one tiny step). Skies: overcast → after-rain → mist → clearing → golden → clear.

**The Descent and Return** (grief, heavy emotion, something that hurts)
Arrive → Settle (extended exhale, self-holding) → Meet (felt sense, RAIN, the Guest House)
→ Turn (honoring: what this pain says about what you love; compassionate presence)
→ Integrate (soothing touch, anchor phrase) → Return (gentle care for tonight).
Skies: dusk → dusk → night → moonlit → moonlit → first-light (or stay in moonlit at night).

**The Crossroads** (a decision, direction, too many options)
Arrive → Settle (resonance breathing) → Meet (values words, the felt sense of each path)
→ Turn (walk each future in the body; a letter from the future self; a council of wise figures via
companion) → Integrate (what is clear now) → Return (the next experiment, not the final answer).
Skies: overcast → mist → clearing → clear → golden → clear.

**The Spark** (flat, numb, unmotivated, low)
Arrive → Upshift (orienting, awaken breath, shake it out, humming) → Meet (what's one thing
that's even slightly interesting?) → Turn (play: documentary narration, an awe moment, a tiny adventure)
→ Savor → Return (one playful act today). Skies: mist → first-light → clearing → clear → golden.
Care: keep it short and light, don't go deep, and never shame the flatness.

**The Untangling** (rumination, loops, a sticky belief)
Arrive → Settle → Meet (write the loop word for word) → Turn (defusion, The Work, externalizing,
view from above) → Integrate (who would you be without it?) → Return (a value-guided step).
Skies: storm → overcast → after-rain → clearing → clear.

**The Homecoming** (self-criticism, shame, "not enough")
Arrive → Settle (soothing touch) → Meet (the critic as a part; what is it protecting?)
→ Turn (a Hakomi probe and/or a letter from a compassionate friend) → Integrate (posture of
self-respect, anchor) → Return (one act of self-respect). Skies: overcast → ember → ember → golden.

**The Harvest** (things are good, a win, a milestone, a quiet day)
Arrive → Savor (taking in the good) → Meet (what made this possible: strengths, people, luck)
→ Turn (meaning: what does this say about who you're becoming?) → Integrate (celebrate in the body)
→ Return (gratitude to someone). Skies: golden → golden → clear → first-light → golden.

**The Threshold** (an ending, a beginning, a transition)
Separation (honor what's ending; what you're leaving) → Liminal (not knowing; sit in the between)
→ Incorporation (who you're becoming; a symbol; a vow). Skies: dusk → night → deep → dawn → first-light.

**The Rest** (burnout, exhaustion, permission)
Arrive → Settle (extended exhale) → NSDR (a long guided body rest) → Probe ("you don't have to
earn rest") → Return (the smallest kindness for tonight). Skies: dusk → night → night → moonlit.
Care: keep it short on words and long on stillness.

## Timing budget

The build tool estimates each movement's length. Trust the estimate, but remember that people take
longer on writing steps than you expect. For a 20-minute experience, aim for **12–16 steps**,
including 2–3 writing steps, 1–2 timed practices and one sit.
