# The Council: lenses from many traditions

Each lens is a way of seeing, with its own question and its own best moves. For every session,
consult **5–7 lenses** that suit the moment, let each say what it sees and what it would offer,
and then **weave** rather than stack. An experience is one coherent passage with a throughline,
not a sampler of techniques.

Good weaving usually pairs:
- one **body** lens (to regulate and ground),
- one or two **heart/mind** lenses (to meet and turn),
- one **meaning or action** lens (to orient and carry it home),
- and one **unexpected** lens: poetry, play, awe, a contemplative tradition. This is often where the
  moment the person remembers comes from.

Represent each tradition faithfully. Ask whether a seasoned practitioner of it would recognize
what you made as their work done well. Credit lineages in the experience's `lineage` list.

---

## Body

**Somatic Experiencing & polyvagal theory** (Peter Levine; Stephen Porges; Deb Dana)
- Asks: *What is the nervous system doing, and what would help it feel safe enough?*
- Moves: orienting (let the eyes find something pleasant), pendulation (move attention between a
  hard place and a resourced one), titration (small doses), self-holding, "voo" sound, completing an
  interrupted impulse (pushing, turning away), noticing resources.
- Best when: activated or shut down; trauma-adjacent material; the body is carrying it.
- Care: go slowly; never push toward overwhelming sensation; always return to a resource.

**Breath science** (cyclic sighing, Balban & Huberman 2023; resonance breathing, Lehrer; extended exhale)
- Asks: *Which rhythm moves this state where it needs to go?*
- Moves: physiological sigh (double inhale, long exhale) for fast downshifting; resonance breathing
  (~5.5 breaths/min) for coherence; humming for vagal tone; brief, gentle energizing breath for flatness.
- Care: no long breath holds or hyperventilation for anyone anxious or prone to panic, pregnant,
  or with heart conditions or epilepsy. Always offer "breathe normally if this doesn't suit you".

**Movement & discharge** (TRE, David Berceli; qigong shaking; 5Rhythms, Gabrielle Roth; yoga)
- Asks: *What does the body want to do?*
- Moves: shaking out, swaying, pushing against a wall, stretching toward the ceiling, walking
  slowly, letting the body choose a posture, "shake it off" for 60 seconds.
- Best when: restless, stuck energy, tired-and-wired, after conflict.

**Yoga nidra / NSDR & body scan** (Satyananda; Jon Kabat-Zinn's MBSR)
- Asks: *Can the body be met part by part, without needing to change?*
- Moves: rotating attention through the body, opposites (heavy/light, warm/cool), intention (sankalpa).
- Best when: depleted, sleepless, evening. Care: can deepen a shutdown state; keep eyes-open options.

## Heart

**Focusing** (Eugene Gendlin; Ann Weiser Cornell's Inner Relationship Focusing)
- Asks: *What is the felt sense of this whole thing, and what does it need?*
- Moves: clearing a space (set each concern down at a distance), finding the felt sense, a "handle"
  (word or image that fits), resonating, asking it, receiving. Cornell's language: "something in me…",
  saying hello to it, keeping it company.
- Best when: vague unease, mixed feelings, a decision the body knows before the head.

**Internal Family Systems** (Richard Schwartz)
- Asks: *Which parts are here, and what are they protecting?*
- Moves: notice a part, get curious ("how do you feel toward it?"), ask what it's afraid would
  happen if it stopped, appreciate its job, find Self qualities (calm, curiosity, compassion,
  clarity, confidence, courage, creativity, connectedness).
- Best when: inner conflict, a harsh critic, procrastination, "part of me wants… part of me…".
- Care: protectors first; never force access to exiles (young, wounded parts) in a solo digital session.

**Self-compassion** (Kristin Neff & Christopher Germer's MSC; Paul Gilbert's Compassion-Focused Therapy)
- Asks: *What would care say right now?*
- Moves: the self-compassion break (this is hard, others feel this too, may I be kind to myself),
  soothing touch, a letter from a compassionate friend, the compassionate other (an ideal caring presence),
  fierce compassion (boundaries, protection) as well as tender.
- Best when: self-criticism, shame, burnout, failure.
- Care: for some, kindness first triggers "backdraft" (old pain arriving). Name that as normal and go slow.

**Nonviolent Communication** (Marshall Rosenberg)
- Asks: *What are you feeling, and what need is alive under it?*
- Moves: feelings & needs vocabulary (words step), translating judgments into needs, self-empathy,
  a request (of self or other).
- Best when: conflict, resentment, confusion about what's wrong.

**Emotion-focused therapy & Gestalt** (Les Greenberg; Fritz & Laura Perls)
- Asks: *What's unfinished, and what happens if it's expressed?*
- Moves: two-chair dialogue (in writing: the critic speaks, then the criticized replies), speaking
  directly to someone absent (an unsent letter), noticing what's happening now, "stay with it".
- Best when: stuck anger or grief, inner conflict, unsaid words.

**Hakomi** (Ron Kurtz)
- Asks: *What happens inside when you hear something nourishing?*
- Moves: the "probe". In mindful stillness, offer a simple sentence ("You are welcome here", "You
  don't have to earn rest") and invite them to notice what happens: relaxing, tightening, tears,
  a thought that argues. Whatever happens is information, not failure. Loving presence throughout.
- Best when: beliefs held in the body; a digital format suits it well (text, a pause, then a choose step).

## Mind

**Acceptance & Commitment Therapy** (Steven Hayes; Russ Harris)
- Asks: *What thoughts are hooking you, and what matters to you anyway?*
- Moves: defusion ("I'm having the thought that…", say it slowly, sing it, leaves on a stream),
  expansion (making room for feelings), values compass, choice point (toward or away), committed action.
- Best when: rumination, avoidance, "I can't until I feel better".

**Inquiry: The Work & cognitive restructuring** (Byron Katie; Aaron Beck; David Burns)
- Asks: *Is that thought true? Who would you be without it?*
- Moves: Katie's four questions + turnarounds; identifying distortions (all-or-nothing,
  mind-reading, fortune-telling); the double standard ("what would you tell a friend?").
- Best when: a specific painful belief ("they don't respect me", "I'm behind").
- Care: never use inquiry to invalidate real harm or injustice.

**Stoicism** (Epictetus, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius; Pierre Hadot)
- Asks: *What is in your control, and what is this asking of your character?*
- Moves: the dichotomy of control (two columns), the view from above, the evening review,
  premeditation (gently), "the obstacle is the way" reframing, memento mori used lightly to clarify priorities.
- Best when: overwhelm by circumstance, frustration, decisions, the need for steadiness.

**Narrative therapy** (Michael White; David Epston)
- Asks: *What story is being told, and what other story is also true?*
- Moves: externalizing ("the Rush", "the Fog": name the problem as separate from the person), its
  tactics, unique outcomes (times it didn't win), re-authoring, an audience of witnesses.
- Best when: identity-level stories ("I'm lazy", "I always…").

**Coherence therapy / memory reconsolidation** (Bruce Ecker)
- Asks: *What emotional truth makes this symptom make sense?*
- Moves: find the hidden purpose ("if I relax, then…"), state it out loud, then hold it next to a
  vivid experience that contradicts it (juxtaposition).
- Best when: "I know better but I still…". Care: slow and respectful; offer it, don't push.

## Meaning & spirit (offered as practices, never as doctrine)

**Existential & logotherapy** (Viktor Frankl; Irvin Yalom; Rollo May)
- Asks: *What is this moment asking of you? Where is meaning, even here?*
- Moves: "what does life ask of me now?", meaning through what we give, what we receive and how we
  meet suffering; facing a given (freedom, aloneness, finitude) with courage.

**Buddhist contemplative practice** (Tara Brach's RAIN; Thich Nhat Hanh; Jack Kornfield; Pema Chödrön; Ajahn Chah)
- Asks: *Can this be met with presence, without grasping or pushing away?*
- Moves: RAIN (Recognize, Allow, Investigate, Nurture), mindful breathing gathas ("breathing in, I
  calm my body…"), loving-kindness (metta), tonglen (breathe in the pain, breathe out relief; not
  for the overwhelmed), noting, impermanence as relief.

**Taoism** (Lao Tzu; Zhuangzi)
- Asks: *Where are you pushing the river?*
- Moves: wu wei (effortless action), water as teacher, the useful emptiness of the bowl, the uncarved
  block, Zhuangzi's playful perspective-shifts (the butterfly dream, the useless tree).
- Best when: striving, forcing, over-control, fatigue from effort.

**Christian contemplative** (Ignatius of Loyola; Lectio Divina; Thomas Keating)
- Asks: *Where was there life today, and where was there drag?*
- Moves: the Examen (consolation and desolation, gratitude, tomorrow), Lectio (read slowly, notice
  the word that shimmers, respond, rest), discernment for decisions. Offer in secular language
  unless the person is religious.

**Jungian** (C.G. Jung; Robert Johnson; James Hillman)
- Asks: *What image or symbol is carrying this?*
- Moves: active imagination (let an image arise and dialogue with it), the shadow (what we disown),
  dream-tending, a personal symbol to carry.

**Poetry as medicine** (David Whyte; John O'Donohue; Mary Oliver; Rilke; Rumi)
- Asks: *What can an image say that analysis can't?*
- Moves: a short poem placed exactly at the turn, a blessing at the close, "the conversational
  nature of reality". **Copyright**: write original verse in the spirit of these poets, or quote
  only public-domain texts (Rilke in the original German, Whitman, Dickinson, older translations of
  Lao Tzu or Rumi). Short attributed lines at most from modern work.

## Life & action

**Solution-focused brief therapy** (Steve de Shazer; Insoo Kim Berg)
- Asks: *What's already working, and what would tomorrow look like if this were better?*
- Moves: the miracle question, exceptions (when is it even slightly less bad?), scaling (0–10 and
  "what would one point higher look like?"), coping questions ("how have you managed so far?").
- Best when: stuck, problem-saturated, wanting change. Light, fast, hopeful.

**Coaching** (Clean Language, David Grove; Co-Active; Motivational Interviewing, Miller & Rollnick)
- Asks: *What would you like to have happen?*
- Moves: clean questions ("and what kind of X is that X?", "and where is that?", "and when X,
  then what happens?"), powerful questions, evoking their own reasons for change (change talk),
  readiness ruler.
- Best when: intake itself, ambivalence, decisions, motivation.

**Behavior design** (BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits; Peter Gollwitzer's implementation intentions; Gabriele Oettingen's WOOP)
- Asks: *What is the smallest real step, and when exactly will it happen?*
- Moves: "After I [anchor], I will [tiny behavior]", WOOP (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan), celebrate
  immediately after the tiny behavior. Most experiences end here.

**Positive psychology** (Martin Seligman; Barbara Fredrickson; Fred Bryant; Rick Hanson; Laura King)
- Asks: *What's good here, and can it be let in more fully?*
- Moves: three good things, savoring, taking in the good (HEAL: Have, Enrich, Absorb, Link),
  best possible self, character strengths in action, gratitude letter.
- Care: never as a bypass for pain. Pair "this is hard" with "and this is also here".

## Connection, wonder, play

**Awe & nature** (Dacher Keltner; shinrin-yoku; Robin Wall Kimmerer)
- Asks: *What is larger than this?*
- Moves: an awe moment (the night sky, deep time, the size of the ocean), a micro-awe walk, looking
  out of a window at the sky as practice, gratitude and reciprocity with the living world.
- Best when: self-preoccupation, rumination, cynicism, smallness.

**The Work That Reconnects** (Joanna Macy)
- Asks: *What do you love, and what do you grieve for, and what will you do?*
- Moves: the spiral: gratitude → honoring our pain for the world → seeing with new eyes → going forth.
- Best when: eco-anxiety, despair about the world, burnout from caring.

**Attachment & interpersonal neurobiology** (Sue Johnson; Daniel Siegel)
- Asks: *Who is with you in this? How do you "feel felt"?*
- Moves: imagining a secure presence, the Wheel of Awareness, recalling a moment of being truly
  understood, a loving-kindness chain (someone who loves you, then you).

**Play & improvisation** (Stuart Brown; Keith Johnstone; Viola Spolin)
- Asks: *Where's the fun? What would lightness do?*
- Moves: narrate your situation like a nature documentary, write your inner critic's job
  advertisement, "yes, and" your situation, give the problem a silly name, a 30-second dance break.
  Play creates distance and flexibility fast. Use it only when there's enough safety for it.

**Expressive arts & writing** (James Pennebaker; Natalie Rogers; Julia Cameron)
- Asks: *What needs to be said, without editing?*
- Moves: expressive writing (4–8 minutes, deepest thoughts and feelings, don't stop), the unsent letter,
  write with the non-dominant hand, a six-word story, list-making, a drawing (on paper beside them).
